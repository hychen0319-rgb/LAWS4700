Reference

      
Glossary of Terms

      
Key legal and technical terms used in this handbook, explained in plain English.

    

    

      A
      
APPAustralian Privacy Principle. A set of rules under the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) that govern how organisations collect, use, store, and disclose personal information.

      
AttributionGiving credit to the original creator of a work. Most open source licences require you to include the original copyright notice when you use the code.

    

    

      C
      
Confidential informationInformation that is not publicly known and is subject to obligations of secrecy, whether through a contract, equity, or statute.

      
CopyleftA type of open source licence, such as the GPL, that requires anyone who distributes modified versions of the software to also make their source code available under the same licence.

      
CopyrightThe legal right to control who can copy, modify, or distribute a creative work. In Australia, it arises automatically when an original work is created. No registration is required.

    

    

      D
      
Duty of confidenceA legal obligation not to misuse information received in circumstances of confidence. Recognised by Australian courts even without a written contract.

    

    

      E
      
Equitable dutyAn obligation recognised by courts of equity, as distinct from statute or contract. The duty of confidence is the most relevant example in this handbook.

    

    

      G
      
GPLGNU General Public Licence. An open source licence that requires you to release the source code of any software that incorporates GPL-licensed code, if you distribute it. Often called a viral licence.

    

    

      I
      
IPIntellectual property. A broad term covering legal rights in creative works (copyright), inventions (patents), brands (trade marks), and confidential information.

      
IP assignment clauseA clause in an employment or contractor agreement that transfers ownership of intellectual property from the creator to the employer or client.

    

    

      L
      
Literary workUnder the Copyright Act 1968 (Cth), any work expressed in words, figures, or symbols, including software source code.

    

    

      M
      
MIT LicenceA permissive open source licence that allows almost unrestricted use, modification, and distribution, as long as the original copyright notice is included.

      
Moral rightsRights that belong to the author of a work personally. These include the right to be named as author and the right not to have the work treated in a degrading way. They cannot be sold or transferred.

    

    

      O
      
OAICOffice of the Australian Information Commissioner. The federal body that regulates privacy and freedom of information in Australia.

      
Open sourceSoftware whose source code is made publicly available under a licence that permits use, modification, and distribution. Different open source licences impose different conditions.

    

    

      P
      
Permissive licenceA type of open source licence, such as MIT or Apache 2.0, that allows use in proprietary software with few restrictions, typically just attribution.

      
Personal informationUnder the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth), any information or opinion about an identified individual, or an individual who is reasonably identifiable.

    

    

      S
      
s 35(6)Section 35(6) of the Copyright Act 1968 (Cth). The rule that gives employers automatic copyright ownership of works created by employees in the course of their employment.

    

    

      T
      
Trade secretConfidential information that has commercial value because it is secret. Protected even after employment ends. Examples include proprietary algorithms, client lists, and pricing models.
